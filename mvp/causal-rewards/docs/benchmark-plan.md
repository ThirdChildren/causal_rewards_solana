# Benchmark Plan — Causal Rewards Protocol (M1)

**Status:** M1 draft. Owner: `causal-inference-engineer`. The benchmark *report* (results at
target scale) is an M4 deliverable; this document is the frozen *plan* it will execute.

This plan defines how we compare **four reward allocation baselines** under **one fixed reward
budget** across **six benchmark scenarios**. The scenarios are generated by the deterministic
DePIN simulator (`simulator/`, package `depin_sim`) and are reproducible from a committed seed.

> **Honesty clause (CLAUDE.md #8).** The goal is a credible, reproducible comparison — *not*
> "causal wins". The NULL-effect and LOW-POWER scenarios are first-class outcomes: if causal
> allocation pays little or nothing there, that is the *correct* answer and we publish it. We do
> not tune the estimator to manufacture positive payouts.

---

## 1. What is being compared

### 1.1 The four reward baselines (one fixed budget `B`)

All four allocators split the **same** fixed budget `B` across cohorts (geographic cluster ×
time-block units). They differ only in the per-cohort weight `w_i`; each cohort receives
`B · w_i / Σ w_j`. Intra-cohort splitting (frozen quality-weighted rule) is identical for all
four and is out of scope for the cross-baseline comparison.

| Baseline    | Per-cohort weight `w_i` | What it rewards |
|-------------|-------------------------|-----------------|
| **activity**  | signed observation count | that work happened |
| **quality**   | quality-weighted observation count | that work met a technical bar |
| **scarcity**  | inverse local supply / sparse-cell indicator | under-supplied areas |
| **causal**    | `conservative_effect_i = max(0, effect_i − z · SE_i)` | measured additional value |

The **causal** weight is produced by the M3 causal engine (cluster-robust effect estimate →
positive-improvement transform → conservative lower bound). A cohort that fails the frozen
minimum-sample rule or whose conservative bound is ≤ 0 gets weight 0 (CLAUDE.md #3).

### 1.2 The estimand (fixed for every scenario)

- **Primary outcome:** out-of-sample prediction error, `held_out_rmse` (lower is better).
- **Unit:** `geo_cohort_time_block` (geographic cohort within a time block) — never a device
  (CLAUDE.md #4; matches `specs/manifest.schema.json` `estimand.unit_type`).
- **Effect:** average change in held-out RMSE caused by including a cohort's data, estimated at
  cohort × time-block with **cluster-robust** SEs, converted to a non-negative improvement
  metric before valuation.

### 1.3 Ground truth (simulator-only)

The simulator knows each cohort's *true* effect. The causal engine does **not**; it must recover
the effect from observed outcomes alone. This separation is what lets the benchmark score
attribution accuracy. True effects, interference, and confounding live only in the simulator
config and never in a real frozen manifest.

---

## 2. Metrics

Reported per scenario, per baseline, all under the same budget `B`:

| Metric | Definition | Direction |
|--------|------------|-----------|
| **Budget efficiency** | realized outcome improvement purchased per unit budget, `Σ_i (true_effect_i · budget_share_i)` (higher = budget flows to cohorts that truly help) | higher better |
| **Redundancy waste** | share of budget paid to cohorts whose *true* conservative value is non-positive (redundant / Sybil / null cohorts) | lower better |
| **Attribution error** | `Σ_i | budget_share_i − true_value_share_i |` (L1 distance between paid shares and true-value shares) | lower better |
| **Sign/confounding error** | budget share sent to cohorts whose naive (correlational) effect has the wrong sign vs. truth | lower better |
| **Coverage response** | ratio of reward concentration in sparse vs. saturated cells | descriptive |
| **Zero-pay rate (causal)** | fraction of cohorts the causal engine pays 0 (min-sample fail or bound ≤ 0) | descriptive — *high is correct under null/low power* |

Secondary metrics are descriptive only unless corrected for multiple testing (CLAUDE.md: one
primary outcome per experiment).

## 3. The six scenarios

Each scenario ships as a committed JSON file in `simulator/scenarios/` and is fully reproducible
from its seed. The numbers below are indicative alpha-scale runs (small cell/block counts);
the M4 report re-runs at target scale (§5). "Naive gap" = raw control-minus-treated mean RMSE,
i.e. what a design-blind correlational read would report; "true effect" = mean true per-cohort
effect. Divergence between them is the point of the scenario.

### S1 — Strong signal (happy path) · `s1_strong_signal.json`
- **Tests:** the well-behaved case — large, clean effect, well-powered, no interference/confounding.
- **DGP assumptions:** `true_effect=0.18`, low noise, cluster-randomized, no Sybils, flat demand.
- **Expected outcome:** causal recovers a positive conservative effect for most cohorts and
  matches or beats activity/quality on budget efficiency. Alpha run: true≈0.145, naive gap≈+0.102.
- **Headline metric:** budget efficiency; attribution error.

### S2 — Null / no additionality · `s2_null_effect.json`
- **Tests:** cohorts that add **zero** value. The canonical honesty scenario.
- **DGP assumptions:** `true_effect=0.0`; everything else as S1.
- **Expected outcome:** **causal pays ≈ 0** (high zero-pay rate) while activity/quality/scarcity
  still spend the whole budget on work that changed nothing. Alpha run: true=0.000, naive gap≈+0.013
  (pure noise). *This is a valid, publishable result, not a failure.*
- **Headline metric:** redundancy waste (activity/quality high, causal ≈ 0); zero-pay rate.

### S3 — Low statistical power / small effect · `s3_low_power.json`
- **Tests:** a **real but small** effect drowned by cohort-level noise and too few clusters/blocks.
- **DGP assumptions:** `true_effect=0.03`, high `cluster_noise_sd=0.15`, few cells (20) & blocks (8),
  lower coverage → many cohort-blocks below the minimum-sample rule (alpha: only ~47/160 qualify).
- **Expected outcome:** the conservative lower bound is ≤ 0 for most cohorts, so **causal pays
  little/nothing** even though a positive effect exists. This is an honest statement about the
  network's power, **not** a bug and **not** something to tune away. Surfaces the #1 statistical risk.
- **Headline metric:** zero-pay rate; and a power note (min detectable effect vs. `true_effect`).

### S4 — Interference / spillover · `s4_interference.json` (+ mitigation `s4b_interference_guardband.json`)
- **Tests:** treating a cohort also lowers **neighbor** cohorts' error, contaminating controls and
  biasing the naive effect. DePIN cohorts interfere; we surface it, not hide it (CLAUDE.md invariant).
- **DGP assumptions:** `interference=0.6` (spillover to grid neighbors), otherwise S1-like.
- **Expected outcome:** the naive/no-guard estimate is **attenuated** (controls improve too), so
  effect is under-estimated; the `s4b` variant applies a 1-cell **guard band** (drops contaminated
  control cohorts, alpha: 480/960 eligible) and recovers a less-biased effect at the cost of sample.
  The benchmark reports bias **and** a sensitivity range, never a single point estimate.
- **Headline metric:** bias (naive vs guard-band effect); attribution error under both.

### S5 — Sybil contamination · `s5_sybil_contamination.json`
- **Tests:** duplicated activity that inflates counts but adds **no information**.
- **DGP assumptions:** 35% of sensors are Sybils in replication groups, `replication_factor=3`
  (alpha: total observations inflate ~50k→90k), true effect unchanged.
- **Expected outcome:** activity (and to a lesser degree quality) **overpay** Sybil-heavy cohorts;
  causal, which scores information value not volume, does not. Sybil-duplicated observations carry
  zero information weight in the cohort quality aggregate.
- **Headline metric:** redundancy waste (share of budget to Sybil-inflated cohorts).

### S6 — Demand-shift confounding · `s6_demand_shift.json`
- **Tests:** demand co-moves with both inclusion propensity and outcome, so a **correlational**
  read mis-attributes demand-driven error changes to the cohort. Uses `observational_replay`, which
  is discovery-only and **not eligible for the strongest causal claim** (concept note §6.3).
- **DGP assumptions:** seasonal demand (`amplitude=0.6`), `confounding=0.5`, non-random inclusion
  propensity tied to cohort info-value.
- **Expected outcome:** the naive gap gets the **wrong sign** vs. truth (alpha: true≈+0.041 but
  naive gap≈−0.032). The benchmark shows that a randomized design (S1) breaks the confound while
  observational replay does not; causal-on-observational is flagged as discovery-grade with an
  explicit unmeasured-confounding sensitivity bound.
- **Headline metric:** sign/confounding error; contrast randomized vs observational on the same DGP.

## 4. Statistical risks surfaced early (from the concept note risk register)

1. **Insufficient statistical power (top risk).** Many real DePIN networks will not generate
   enough independent cross-cohort variation for a conservative lower bound to clear zero. S2 and
   S3 are designed to make this visible and publishable. Mitigations we will test: larger time
   windows, matched clusters, pooled analysis. **Residual limitation:** some networks simply
   cannot support a strong causal claim — we say so plainly rather than lowering the bar.
2. **Interference / spillover (top design risk).** Standard independent-unit assumptions fail when
   neighbors interfere. S4/S4b test cluster assignment + guard bands + (for switchback) carryover
   windows, and every interference result is reported with a bias/sensitivity range, never a bare
   point estimate. **Residual limitation:** complex physical networks retain unmodelled spillover.

These two are the risks most likely to make causal allocation **lose** to a simpler baseline in a
realistic scenario. Per CLAUDE.md #8 that is an acceptable, reportable outcome.

---

## 5. Scale

- **Alpha (M1, now):** small scenarios (tens of cells, thousands of observations) chosen for fast,
  auditable, deterministic runs. Sufficient to prove reproducibility and exercise every scenario.
- **Target (M4 report):** **10,000 virtual sensors, 250 cohorts, 1,000,000 signed observations**,
  four baselines under one fixed budget, across all six scenarios. Same simulator, same seeds,
  scaled config. Determinism (identical content hash on same seed + pinned container) must hold at
  scale exactly as it does in the alpha.

---

## 6. Reproducibility

Every scenario run emits a canonical content hash (see `simulator/README.md`,
`simulator/src/depin_sim/canonical.py`) computed under the protocol's float-free canonical
serialization (`specs/serialization.md`). The benchmark report will publish, for each scenario:
the scenario file, the committed seed, the container digest, the content hash, and the per-baseline
metric table — so any third party can reproduce the exact numbers and the ranking.

## 7. Reward-curve fixture (decoupled from the spec example) — Condition 3

The at-scale benchmark uses a **separate manifest fixture** for its reward curve, distinct from
`specs/examples/manifest.example.json`. The spec example is the frozen conformance artifact (its curve
golden `sha256:14b0ec34…` does not move under the v1.2 "Reading B" decision); the benchmark curve is
the **recommended shipped-scale + 10%`B` saturating-cap default** (`reward_curve_hash
sha256:eeab732e…`; recalibration was evaluated and REJECTED under Condition 1 on under-deployment
grounds — `docs/compensation-study.md`). Binding the benchmark to the frozen example would either
force a spec-golden move on every retune or publish OLD-curve numbers under a NEW recommendation —
both incoherent. So they are decoupled.

**Fixture contract** (normative source: `specs/v1.2-migration-proposal.md` "Benchmark manifest
fixture"; `protocol-architect` defines it, `causal-inference-engineer` creates + wires it):

- **Path:** `causal-engine/fixtures/benchmark-manifest.json` (a benchmark input, NOT a spec artifact;
  never a spec golden; exempt from the manifest-golden regeneration protocol).
- **Content:** the structure of `manifest.example.json` with exactly two deltas — the recommended
  shipped-scale + 10%`B` saturating `reward_curve`
  (`[["0","0"],["50000","10000000000"],["100000","10000000000"],["500000","10000000000"],["1000000","10000000000"]]`
  for `B = 100e9`; general rule: shipped scale, ceiling = 10% of `B'`), and its `reward_curve_hash`
  recomputed by `verifier-cli/reference/canonical.py`. The `budget → curve-output` scale is UNCHANGED
  (no recalibration).
- **Loading:** `studies.py::run_study` MUST read this fixture, NOT the spec example. The two diverge
  by construction; that divergence is the point.
- **Hashes:** the fixture's own `reward_curve_hash` = `sha256:eeab732e…` (FINALIZED) and its
  whole-manifest content hash are FIXTURE hashes (they move whenever the benchmark curve is retuned,
  with no spec-version bump), recorded in the benchmark report, not in `specs/manifest.golden.md`.
- **Provenance line:** the benchmark report MUST carry, next to every metric table, the curve-source
  line specified in `docs/benchmark-report.md` "Curve provenance", so a reader sees exactly which
  curve produced the published numbers.

Breakpoints, cap ceiling, and the fixture `reward_curve_hash` are FINALIZED by the Condition-1
compensation study (`docs/compensation-study.md`): shipped scale, per-cohort ceiling = 10% of `B`,
`reward_curve_hash sha256:eeab732e…`. Recalibration was evaluated and rejected on under-deployment
grounds — the shipped `budget → curve-output` scale is kept unchanged.
